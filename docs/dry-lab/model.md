For APOE4 editing in Alzheimer's disease research, how can a programmable editor retain useful target activity while reducing unintended RNA editing?

The PUF recognition domain, the APOBEC catalytic domain and the surrounding RNA sequence each contribute a different part of the design problem. The working premise is that **specificity must be interpreted together with retained activity and the scope of measurement**.

**Reliable RNA-seq data underpin the entire workflow.** We iteratively refined parameter handling, read-quality checks, background screening and transcript orientation across 36 libraries. Excluding known matches from four HEK293T SNP catalogues helped distinguish editor-associated signals from genetic variation. Consistent coverage criteria and agreement across four treated biological replicates produced a traceable candidate-site resource for downstream analysis and model-label assessment.

**PUF modeling connected predictions to experimentally useful variants.** Before building new PUF constructs, we used model predictions to select five candidates for wet-lab testing. We then built and tested those candidates, using the measured outcomes to guide the next modeling iteration. Within that panel, the double-repeat SNE construct increased C388 by **7.77 percentage points**, while reducing C295 and C871 by **13.15 and 34.90 points**, respectively, relative to its matched control. Subsequent grouped evaluation of the PUF-ESM Specificity Ranker reached **Spearman ρ = 0.405** across 66 constructs. Its predicted Top30 recovered **20 of the measured Top30**, compared with 13.64 expected from random selection. In the separate RNA-seq comparison, GVE and SNE retained **97.0% and 66.0% fewer candidate sites** than PUF12, respectively. Both retained apparent C388 signals, although these include endogenous T. Together, these measurements connect favorable three-site profiles with narrower transcriptome-wide candidate sets, while keeping the experimental panels distinct.

**APOBEC engineering completed a structure-to-experiment feedback loop.** Structural hypotheses guided single-mutant screening, experimental measurements informed combination design, and follow-up assays tested the resulting leads. The lead combination achieved a **target-to-comparison-site ratio of 2.156 while retaining 77.3% of parental target activity**, meeting both engineering criteria in the same batch. These results established a concrete route for improving substrate preference while preserving useful activity.

**LAMAR narrowed the search for off-target sites.** Human-transcript adaptation and cytidine ranking translated RNA-seq-supported patterns into shortlists for follow-up. In the reported Stage II test summary, the top 10 and top 20 candidates contained a supported site in **77.9% and 92.1% of 140 positive regions**, respectively. The median best rank was **four**. These regional ranking results define a practical screening objective; the complete two-stage workflow still requires evaluation on newly measured sites. Extending this framework to additional cell lines and species is a future direction, with context-specific data needed to establish transfer performance.

**Toward RNA editing for Alzheimer's disease.** Published work has demonstrated PUF–APOBEC-mediated editing of APOE transcripts in cells ([Han et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-025-64748-6)). ORCA connects that disease-relevant direction to a measurable engineering objective: retain intended RNA editing while reducing unwanted changes elsewhere. By linking experimental feedback, transcriptome-wide assessment and candidate prioritization, our modeling framework supports the selection of editors for future disease-model studies. Therapeutic efficacy will require direct testing in those models.

1. **[Measure the editing profile](#module1).** Module 1 compares target signals and transcriptome-wide candidate sites under a common screening procedure. This gives the project an evidence base for distinguishing reduced unwanted editing from a general loss of activity.
2. **[Choose PUF variants](#module2).** Module 2 ranks recognition-domain variants against the measured balance between C388 and two comparison sites. Structure-conditioned sequence design provides additional substitutions to investigate.
3. **[Engineer APOBEC preference](#module3).** Module 3 uses protein–RNA structures to propose catalytic-domain changes, then assesses substrate preference alongside retained activity. Experimental results determine which designs remain useful leads.
4. **[Prioritize further site validation](#module4).** Module 4 adapts LAMAR to rank RNA regions and cytidines using RNA-seq-derived labels. Its intended output is a manageable candidate list for off-target testing.

The connection between modules is the evidence used to make the next decision. A low candidate-site count needs a retained target signal to be useful; a promising protein score needs measured editing; and a high-ranking RNA site needs experimental confirmation. PUF and APOBEC modeling provide complementary routes to editor improvement. Transcriptome-wide measurement and site prioritization supply the broader assessment needed beyond the three-site assay. For APOE4 editing in Alzheimer's disease research, the next design decision therefore depends on evidence of both retained activity and unwanted RNA changes.
