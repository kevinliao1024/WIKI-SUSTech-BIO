For APOE4 editing in Alzheimer's disease research, how can a programmable editor retain useful target activity while reducing unintended RNA editing?

The PUF recognition domain, the APOBEC catalytic domain and the surrounding RNA sequence each contribute to the design problem. Our goal is to **improve specificity while preserving useful target activity**. Within ORCA, RNA-seq establishes candidate editing evidence, while PUF design and APOBEC engineering evaluate changes to the editor. LAMAR supplies the complementary RNA-site ranking for follow-up. At the system scale, delivery, expression and RNA editing are connected in our [Whole-body Delivery Model](/software#rewire).

**Reliable RNA-seq data underpin the entire workflow.** Using 36 RNA-seq libraries generated by our wet-lab experiments, we iteratively refined parameter handling, read-quality checks, background screening and transcript orientation. Excluding known matches from four HEK293T SNP catalogues helped distinguish editor-associated signals from genetic variation. Consistent coverage criteria and agreement across four treated biological replicates produced a candidate-site resource for downstream analysis.

**PUF modeling connected predictions to experimentally useful variants.** Before building new PUF constructs, we used model predictions to select five candidates for wet-lab testing. We then built and tested those candidates, using the measured outcomes to guide the next modeling iteration. Within that panel, the double-repeat SNE construct increased C388 by **7.77 percentage points**, while reducing C295 and C871 by **13.15 and 34.90 points**, respectively, relative to its matched control. Subsequent grouped evaluation of the PUF-ESM Specificity Ranker reached **Spearman ρ = 0.405** across 66 constructs. Its predicted Top30 recovered **20 of the measured Top30**, compared with 13.64 expected from random selection. In the separate RNA-seq comparison, GVE and SNE retained **97.0% and 66.0% fewer candidate sites** than PUF12, respectively. Both retained apparent C388 signals, which include endogenous T. These measurements connect favorable three-site profiles with narrower transcriptome-wide candidate sets.

**APOBEC engineering completed a structure-to-experiment feedback loop.** Structural hypotheses guided single-mutant screening, experimental measurements informed combination design, and follow-up assays tested the resulting leads. The lead combination achieved a **target-to-comparison-site ratio of 2.156 while retaining 77.3% of parental target activity**, meeting both engineering criteria in the same batch. These results established a concrete route for improving substrate preference while preserving useful activity.

**LAMAR narrowed the search for off-target sites.** Human-transcript adaptation and cytidine ranking translated RNA-seq-supported patterns into shortlists for follow-up. In Stage II testing, the top 10 and top 20 candidates contained a supported site in **77.9% and 92.1% of 140 positive windows**, respectively. The median best rank was **four**. These regional rankings define a practical screening budget.

**Whole-body modeling connects editor design to delivery and tissue coverage.** A compartmental ODE model follows delivery, tissue distribution, blood–brain barrier transport, cellular expression and multisite RNA reactions. Dose scans, sensitivity analysis and parameter uncertainty analysis examine how these processes shape APOE RNA products and unwanted editing. Complementary spatial simulations examine how editing varies with distance from blood vessels. Together, these models help compare delivery and editing assumptions and identify processes for experimental follow-up.

1. **Measure the editing profile.** Module 1 compares target signals and transcriptome-wide candidate sites under a common screening procedure. This gives the project an evidence base for distinguishing reduced unwanted editing from a general loss of activity.
2. **Choose PUF variants.** Module 2 ranks recognition-domain variants against the measured balance between C388 and two comparison sites. Structure-conditioned sequence design provides additional substitutions to investigate.
3. **Engineer APOBEC preference.** Module 3 uses protein–RNA structures to propose catalytic-domain changes, then assesses substrate preference alongside retained activity. Experimental results determine which designs remain useful leads.
4. **Prioritize further site validation.** Module 4 adapts LAMAR to rank RNA regions and cytidines using RNA-seq-derived labels. Its intended output is a manageable candidate list for off-target testing.
5. **Connect molecular design to system-level performance.** The Whole-body Delivery Model brings transport, expression, multisite RNA reactions and tissue-scale behavior into a common decision framework. Evaluating intended-product retention, unwanted editing and coverage together reveals trade-offs across these processes. For ORCA, this complements the editor and site selection in Modules 1–4 and helps compare complete delivery and editing strategies.

<h3 id="module4-therapeutic-window">Toward RNA editing for Alzheimer's disease</h3>

<p data-literature="L-H L-F">Published PUF–APOBEC work demonstrated editing of APOE reporters in HEK293T cells and endogenous APOE3 transcripts in HepG2 cells (Han et al., 2025; correction, 2026). Together with the association between APOE genotype and Alzheimer's disease risk (Farrer et al., 1997), these findings motivate disease-relevant RNA-editing research.</p>

<p data-claim="T-A">ORCA's contribution is an integrated route for selecting editors with retained target signal and a narrower detected RNA profile.</p>
