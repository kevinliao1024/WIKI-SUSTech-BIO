Recognition length requires evaluation in the complete editor

Under the common screening procedure, PUF12 retained 12,908 candidate sites compared with 2,774 for PUF10. Their respective retained-set median rates were 13.99% and 12.75%. The longer recognition construct therefore accompanied a broader detected profile.

Target signal and candidate-site breadth jointly guide recognition and catalytic design.

Our central conclusion is that increasing PUF length does not guarantee a narrower editing profile in the complete fusion editor. The specificity gain reported for an eight-to-ten-repeat REWIRE comparison cannot be assumed to continue from ten to twelve repeats (Han et al., 2022). Repeat number must be interpreted together with scaffold geometry, RNA recruitment and catalytic context preference.

The proposed balance between base-specific recognition and more permissive RNA contacts could explain why additional repeats have different effects across editor configurations. It remains a hypothesis about the underlying mechanism, whereas the broader PUF12 editing profile is an observed result. Higher site-level editing rates and stronger binding affinity are distinct claims; only the former is assessed by these RNA-seq measurements.

Shared-site rates clarify the count comparison

Among 2,221 jointly retained sites, PUF12 had a higher reported rate at 73.1%, with a median within-site increase of 3.02 percentage points. This comparison links rates at the same genomic allele keys within the jointly retained subset.

This shared-site comparison adds information beyond the medians of two different retained-site sets. PUF12's broader profile was accompanied by higher editing at most jointly detected sites, linking editing breadth with increased output within the shared subset. The relationship is descriptive and does not identify the molecular source of the increase.

Smaller candidate sets also accompanied weaker shared-site rates. For 132D, all 195 sites shared with PUF12 had a lower rate, with a median difference of −18.86 percentage points. Retaining target signal while narrowing the profile

APOBEC-containing DNA base-editor studies reported reduced RNA off-target editing alongside retained DNA on-target activity (Grünewald et al., 2019; Zhou et al., 2019). They place control of unwanted RNA editing within an established engineering objective.

GVE retained 383 candidate sites, approximately 97.0% fewer than PUF12, alongside an apparent C388 signal of 64.98%. This combination makes GVE a lead for evaluating a narrower editing profile with retained target signal in HEK293T. Together with SNE’s higher apparent C388 profile, it defines complementary starting points for recognition-domain selection.

Within the BE3 study, wild-type constructs produced 90,509–192,088 RNA C-to-U sites, while BE3-R33A/K34A produced 7–26 (Grünewald et al., 2019). This within-study reduction provides a precedent for engineering RNA specificity. ORCA addresses that objective through joint measurement of target signal and candidate profiles.

Local motif conservation and transcriptome-wide selectivity describe different properties

The recurrent −1 guanine pattern and the differing candidate-site counts can coexist because local catalytic context and RNA recruitment describe different aspects of editing. We interpret the shared motif as consistent with a contribution from the engineered APOBEC domain, whose sequence preferences depend on its scaffold (Han et al., 2025). The GVE shift also shows why recognition-domain effects cannot be dismissed.

Consequently, a conserved motif alone does not imply unchanged transcriptome-wide selectivity, just as fewer candidate sites alone do not establish preserved target activity. Together with the local assays in Modules 2 and 3, these readouts connect recognition, catalytic preference and site prioritization.
