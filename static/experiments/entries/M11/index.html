D. Review and export 7. Inspect amplification and melt curves together with NTC, no-RT and positive controls before accepting Cq values. Check technical-replicate agreement and use consistent baseline/threshold rules within each assay. Investigate discrepant wells; retain the reason for any exclusion. 8. Export raw run data, Cq values, amplification and melt curves, plate map and dilution records. For relative expression, normalize to validated reference genes and the planned biological comparator; use comparative Cq calculations only after confirming compatible amplification efficiencies. [2] p.s. Prepare NTC wells before handling template stocks. Use dedicated clean-area pipettes and fresh aerosol-resistant filter tips for every transfer; keep amplified material away from setup. Keep no-RT and NTC controls distinct. An NTC tests qPCR setup background; a no-RT control tests whether the RNA preparation can generate signal without reverse transcription. Do not open amplified plates in the pre-PCR area. Keep seals intact when archiving or disposing of them to limit carryover into later runs. Expected Outcome Obtain interpretable amplification curves and an assay-consistent melt peak in valid positive samples, with appropriate negative controls and reproducible technical wells. A single melt peak supports specificity but does not prove amplicon identity. Retain control outcomes with the reported expression or quantity results. M11 | v1.3 4
