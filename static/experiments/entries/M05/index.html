Before You Start Choose the host and route Use Trans5α for routine cloning and TransStbl3 for constructs prone to unwanted recombination. Stbl3 host choice does not make a large or unstable construct eligible for Route B. [1, 2] Route Choose when Key sequence A. Conventional Default for new assemblies, low-copy or DNA addition → ice incubation → 42°C large plasmids, non-Amp selection, or when heat shock → ice → recovery → plating colony recovery is important B. Rapid Amp resistance AND high copy number AND DNA addition → 42°C, 2 min → 0°C, 3 a non-large plasmid; a familiar, readily min → direct plating transformed construct Route B is a laboratory-adapted shortcut; its exact program and efficiency are not established by the cited manuals. Choose Route A if plasmid size, copy number or rapid-route suitability is uncertain. [3] Prepare DNA, plates and equipment Per 50 µL cells, start with 1-5 µL containing 1-10 ng purified plasmid, or 1-5 µL completed assembly reaction. Apply any stricter assembly-kit input limit; avoid adding excess reaction mixture. [3] Warm selection plates to room temperature and remove condensation. For Amp plates, use 100 µg/mL final (1:1000 from 100 mg/mL stock). Prepare antibiotic-free medium and a 42°C water bath for Route A. [5] For Route B, program 42°C for 2 min, then 0°C for 3 min, once, without a high-temperature lid setting. If the instrument cannot reach 0°C, transfer immediately after heat shock to an ice-water bath for 3 min; record this substitution rather than replacing 0°C with 4°C. When establishing the method or troubleshooting, include a compatible control plasmid and a no-DNA control. An assembly backbone-only control helps identify cloning background. Procedure Common setup 1. Thaw the required competent-cell aliquots on ice. Use 50 µL per transformation as the reference scale and handle gently without vortexing or repeated pipetting. [1, 2] 2. Add the selected DNA input to the cells and mix by gentle tapping. Use a prechilled PCR tube for Route B. Continue with either A or B below, then complete the common plating stage. Route A. Conventional heat-shock transformation 3. Keep the DNA-cell mixture on ice for 30 min with Trans5α or 20-30 min with TransStbl3. [1, 2] 4. Heat-shock in a 42°C water bath for 45 s, then transfer immediately to ice for 2 min. Keep the tube still during heat shock and cooling. [1, 2] 5. Add 500 µL sterile, antibiotic-free LB. Recover at 37°C, 200 rpm for 1 h, then proceed to plating. [1, 2] M05 | v1.1 2

Route B. Rapid transformation for eligible Amp plasmids 6. Immediately after DNA addition, run the thermocycler program below. Omit the prolonged pre-heat-shock ice incubation. Stage Temperature Time Heat shock 42°C 2 min Cooling 0°C 3 min 7. After cooling, proceed directly to the Amp plate. Do not add a liquid recovery incubation. Plate the approximately 50 µL cell-DNA mixture, or a smaller portion if dense growth is expected. This route is limited to Amp-resistant, high-copy, non-large plasmids. Common plating and incubation 8. For Route A, plate 50-100 µL recovered culture on a labeled selection plate; use an additional plate for more culture if colony yield may be low. For Route B, use the direct-plating amount above. Spread evenly with a sterile spreader, avoiding damage to the agar. [3] 9. Allow the liquid to absorb, invert the plates and incubate at 37°C overnight. If the specific construct has a documented lower-temperature propagation requirement, follow that requirement instead and allow sufficient time for colonies to appear. [1, 2] 10. Inspect the sample and control plates together. Select well-isolated colonies for M06 and subsequent construct verification; colony growth alone does not establish the correct insert or sequence. p.s. Ampicillin is a bactericidal β-lactam. Omitting liquid recovery for suitable Amp-resistant transformants does not mean Amp is non-killing and should not be generalized to other antibiotics. [3, 4] Use freshly thawed cells and avoid repeated freeze-thaw cycles. Keep the cell-DNA mixture cold until its selected heat-shock step. [1, 2] Keep each route's heat-shock device, timing, tube geometry and volume consistent. Do not substitute Route B's 2 min thermocycler step for Route A's 45 s water-bath step alone. Plate a dilution or use another plate if colonies would be crowded. Avoid prolonged incubation: tiny colonies around larger Amp colonies may be satellites, not independent clones. [5] Expected Outcome Expect isolated sample colonies, positive-control growth and little or no no-DNA-control growth. Yield varies with DNA, cells and route; equivalent efficiency is not assumed. Record the host, route, DNA input, antibiotic and temperature substitutions. Screen and sequence candidate colonies before confirming construct identity. M05 | v1.1 3
