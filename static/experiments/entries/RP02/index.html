Target Reporter Construction by Golden Gate Assembly RP02 | ver 1.0 | Revised 2026-10-13 SUSTech-BIO 2026 iGEM Team Purpose Insert a target-site recognition window into a compatible universal reporter by annealing the synthetic DNA oligos designed in RP01 and performing a one-pot BsaI digestion-ligation reaction. Construct WT, AC, and INAC separately, then identify clones by sequence analysis. This guide uses Invitrogen GeneArt Type IIs Assembly Kit, Bsa I (A15917). The direct short-oligo route and the reporter's custom stuffer are adaptations; the manufacturer's published fragment-size ranges and performance claims do not establish efficiency for this reporter. The quantities below are starting conditions for method establishment, not a reported team optimization. Before You Start Use the oligos designed by the reader in RP01, rather than treating the APOE4 example as a universal target. Check the ordered sequences and modifications against the design file. For the reference acceptor, the forward strand starts CAGC and the reverse strand starts TGCC; neither oligo needs an added BsaI recognition site. [3] Scan the complete physical acceptor's reference for GGTCTC and GAGACC, including the circular origin. Confirm that extra retained sites have been removed in the actual plasmid. The 2,027-bp cassette alone cannot certify a whole plasmid; the revised 4,669-bp file is a computational design, not sequencing evidence for a repaired clone. [3] Prepare separate labeled tubes and expected assembled references for WT, AC, and INAC. Include a backbone-only assembly control when establishing the method. A known plasmid transformation control helps distinguish assembly failure from poor cell competency. This acceptor replaces a custom stuffer and has no ccdB counter-selection. Screen resulting colonies by sequence; the kit's control-vector color selection does not apply to the reporter. The Thermo manual specifies pre-cloned inserts from 25 bp and direct PCR/synthetic fragments from 250 bp; it does not validate the RP01 example's 15-nt core as a directly annealed insert. [1] Procedure A. Anneal each oligo pair 1. Resuspend each strand to 100 µM in the annealing buffer. Calculate the required volume from the provider's stated amount: volume in µL = 1,000 x amount in nmol / concentration in µM. Prepare equal molar concentrations for the paired strands. [4] 2. For each variant, mix 1 µL forward oligo, 1 µL reverse oligo, and 18 µL annealing buffer. The 20-µL mixture contains 100 pmol of each strand and can yield a nominal 5 µM duplex stock. This is a preparation scale, not a measured annealing yield. 3. Heat to 94°C for 2 minutes, then allow gradual cooling to room temperature in a switched-off heat block. Keep the paired tube in the block during cooling rather than transferring it directly to ice. [4] 4. Dilute 2 µL of the annealed stock with 98 µL of EDTA-free 10 mM Tris-HCl, pH 8.0, to make a nominal 100 nM working solution. Keep unused annealed stock in small aliquots at -20°C. [1,4] B. Set up the one-pot assembly 5. Prepare the sequence-compatible acceptor at 75 ng/µL in water or EDTA-free 10 mM Tris-HCl, pH 8.0. Thaw the required enzyme mix, mix gently, and keep it on ice. The assembly kit does not phosphorylate synthetic DNA ends. Both ordered strands must already carry the RP01 5' phosphate; unmodified oligos require a separate phosphorylation step before entering this route. [4] Dilute short duplexes by molarity. Adding the same mass of a short insert and a several-kilobase vector can create a large insert excess. Keep WT, AC, and INAC separate through annealing, assembly, transformation, and sequencing. Their small sequence differences can be missed if tube names or clone IDs are exchanged. Use sequencing to discriminate short target inserts. The RP01 example differs only slightly in length from the acceptor stuffer; routine agarose sizing alone is not an adequate identity check. A revised sequence file does not modify a stored plasmid. Obtain a physical acceptor carrying the intended synonymous substitutions and verify those sites before using it in a BsaI reaction. [3] Expected Outcome Obtain separately identified WT, AC, and INAC clones with the intended insert and both junctions supported by readable sequence data. For the revised 4,669-bp design and the original RP01 15-nt cores, simulated products are 4,645 bp and contain no BsaI recognition sites; recalculate for the reader's own designs. [3] The backbone-only control indicates cloning background. Colony growth or sequence correctness does not establish fluorescence behavior or RNA-editing activity, which require downstream experiments.
