Before You Start Prepare separate 10 µM primer working solutions. For a 100 µM stock, mix 10 µL stock with 90 µL water. The reaction table assumes this working concentration. Check the expected amplicon length and both primer sequences. A known amplifiable positive control may be included when establishing an assay. Thaw and gently mix the chosen premix, then briefly spin. Assemble reactions on ice in a clean area separated from amplified DNA. Prepare separate mixes when comparing products. Procedure A. Prepare the shared reaction mixture 1. Select one master mix from Materials & Equipment. Calculate template volume V (µL) = required DNA mass (ng) / measured concentration (ng/µL), using the product-specific input table below. Dilute concentrated stocks to a reliably pipettable volume. 2. Prepare reactions with the shared recipe. For multiple samples using the same enzyme, primer pair and template volume, prepare a mixture without template, allowing about 10% extra for pipetting loss. Dispense it before adding templates. *Start at 60 °C. For primers >20 nt, use the lower primer Tm + 3 °C; for primers <20 nt, use the lower Tm. For exactly 20 nt, start at the default and optimize if needed. Complex targets or fragments >10 kb may need 15 s/kb. [2] C. Inspect the product 5. Analyze a small aliquot alongside an appropriate DNA ladder and any positive control included. Select agarose concentration for the expected fragment size and add loading dye at its working dilution. Retain the remaining product for the chosen downstream workflow. See M02 for the gel check and interpretation. p.s. Label tubes with the master mix and program name. When changing enzyme, reload its complete program, including initial and final stages. Use the stated 10 µM primer working solutions. Adding a 100 µM stock at the listed volumes gives a tenfold concentration error. Recalculate water when template volume changes. For inconsistent repeats, check mixing, pipetting and seals; use a shared reaction mix. Expected Outcome A predominant product at the expected size supports successful amplification. Save the gel image with lane identities. Band size alone does not establish sequence identity. Troubleshooting For problems first seen on a gel, start with M02, then use PCR Optimization below if indicated. PCR Optimization Use these options after M02 has checked sample loading, electrophoresis and detection. A gel pattern suggests which check to make; it does not identify a cause by itself. Keep the selected enzyme-specific recipe and program as the starting point, and change one factor at a time. O1. Low or absent amplification Check primer identity, sequences, orientation and binding to the actual template. Confirm the 10 µM working dilution. If primer stock quality is suspect, reconstitute an unused dry aliquot and prepare a fresh working solution. Review template integrity, purity and input against the selected product guidance. [1, 2, 4] Recheck the full matching thermal program, especially annealing and length-dependent extension. Use an annealing-temperature gradient within the selected enzyme guidance if needed. For ABclonal difficult or long targets, apply its extension guidance in Section B; do not transfer settings between enzymes. A dilution comparison can help assess suspected template inhibition, but also reduces template input. [1-4] If a distinct, correctly sized product is present but more DNA is needed, run additional parallel PCRs and pool matching products for purification. This means additional reactions, not an automatic increase in cycle number. A faint, well-separated target can be recovered using M02; quantify the eluate to decide whether it meets the downstream requirement. O2. Nonspecific amplification or short by-products After M02 excludes poor separation or loading distortion, review the expected product map and primer specificity. Extra bands or a short band near the front are candidates for nonspecific amplification or primer dimers, respectively; position alone does not establish their identity. Confirm primer working dilution and template input before testing annealing conditions. Follow the selected enzyme guidance: for PrimeSTAR Max, retain the specified short annealing duration; for ABclonal, evaluate annealing temperature and excessive primer/template input or cycling when appropriate. Redesign primers if the binding sites are unsuitable. Recover the intended band in M02 only if it is clearly separated. [1-3] O3. Residual plasmid template Consider residual template separately from newly amplified by-products when the PCR started from a plasmid. Uncut plasmid conformations can migrate differently from linear DNA, so apparent size alone cannot identify a residual-template band. For residual Dam-methylated plasmid template containing DpnI recognition sites, see the DpnI digestion chapter. DpnI does not generally remove unmethylated nonspecific PCR amplicons; use O2 for those products. Confirm template methylation and site suitability before selecting digestion. [5] M01 | v1.6 5
