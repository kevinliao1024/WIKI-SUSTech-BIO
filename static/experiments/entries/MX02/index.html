Target Transcript RT-PCR and Sequencing Readout MX02 | ver 1.2 | Revised 2026-10-14 SUSTech-BIO 2026 iGEM Team Purpose This integrated workflow combines the core molecular biology protocols to amplify a defined target-transcript region from purified RNA and obtain bidirectional Sanger traces or reference-based Nanopore amplicon data. It links M08 or M09 (RNA extraction), M10 (DNA removal and reverse transcription), M01 (endpoint PCR of cDNA) and M02 (gel analysis and conditional product recovery), with MR01 available as an alternative RNA-extraction route. MX02 explains the handoffs between these modules and the requirements for sequencing submission and initial readout, using APOE4 and OpenA-1 as examples; sequence signals require suitable controls and analysis before they can support an RNA-editing claim. Record the image, lane identities and observed bands; retain the bulk PCR reaction in its original labeled tube. 8. Use the unpurified-PCR route when a distinct, correctly sized target band is present without substantial competing products. If multiple products, strong short by-products or smearing prevent confident submission, first check shared gel issues in M02, then use M01 Optimization as appropriate. Recover the intended band with M02 only when size selection is necessary; update the submission type if the product is purified. 9. If more volume is required, perform additional matching PCRs and pool only products from the same cDNA sample, primer pair and program. Preserve their relationship to the parent sample. Do not pool different biological samples or add cycles solely to satisfy a volume requirement. D. Submit for the selected sequencing branch 10. For Sanger, submit the unpurified product as a PCR sample and request separate forward and reverse reads. Provide each custom primer separately, labeled with its name and 5′ to 3′ sequence; the working concentration is 10 µM. For each sequencing reaction use one primer, not an F/R mixture. Keep the provider's order-to-sample mapping with the experiment. The team generally submits unpurified PCR products. Any provider cleanup or library preparation belongs to the commissioned service; it is not a separately documented team laboratory step. E. Receive data and perform the initial readout 12. Archive original Sanger AB1 files and sequence calls, or Nanopore reference FASTA, reads/alignment files, variant tables and fragment-length outputs. Preserve the submitted reference, primer sequences, order mapping and processing software/settings alongside these files. Do not overwrite original files with edited sequences. 13. Align the sequence data to the sample-specific amplicon reference and orient both directions consistently. Verify the target coordinate and flanking sequence before interpreting a secondary base. A C-to-U RNA change appears as C-to-T in forward cDNA data or G-to-A in a reverse trace. 14. For Sanger, inspect target-region trace quality and the matched background in both directions. If reporting a peak-composition index, identify the extraction method and report F T/(C+T) and R A/(G+A) separately. Directional disagreement and failed site quality must remain visible; these indices are not automatically calibrated RNA-edited fractions. 15. For Nanopore, evaluate quality-filtered reads aligned at the specified target, with mapping/base-quality filters and the usable depth recorded. A C/T composition can be summarized as T/(C+T), explicitly stating that A/G calls are outside this denominator. Keep raw and comparator values; do not turn low coverage or failed assay checks into zero editing. Consensus sequence or a simulated AB1 alone cannot quantify an edited fraction. The initial readout establishes product identity and a candidate target signal. Without suitable RNA-origin controls and assay qualification, report a sequence/peak signal or apparent C/T read composition with its limitations. Native or other allele contributions, PCR bias and sequencing error may affect interpretation. Detailed validated fraction estimation belongs to a separately specified analysis method. p.s. Use the actual ordered primer sequence, not only a reused primer name. The APOE forward-primer length difference and the OpenA-1 redesign show why the name alone is insufficient. The 1.2 µL cDNA input is 4.8% of the 25 µL reaction, within the PCR reagent's less-than-10% carryover guidance. Do not substitute the 1:5 cDNA dilution used in a separate qPCR plan. [2] GENEWIZ's Sanger AB1 and a Nanopore provider's simulated AB1 are different data products. Use real Sanger traces for chromatogram-based indices, and read/alignment evidence for Nanopore composition. [3, 4] DNA removal is part of the RT workflow, but it does not demonstrate that a particular sample is free of residual plasmid. Keep no-RT evidence when making an RNA-origin claim; do not substitute cloning DpnI treatment as proof. Keep culture-well IDs through RNA, RT, PCR and sequencing. Expected Outcome A distinct product at the sample-reference size, with usable sequence covering the target and the expected flanking region. APOE4 and OpenA-1 references predict 951 bp and 523 bp respectively; a band alone is not sequence confirmation. For a newly controlled run, no-RT and NTC should not show a target-sized product. A positive assay control helps interpret a sample without amplification. Retain the original gel and sequencing data even when a sample fails these checks. Obtain bidirectional Sanger traces or Nanopore read/alignment evidence suitable for an explicitly defined initial target readout. Missing historical controls and uncalibrated peak ratios remain limitations, not evidence of RNA-only origin or exact editing efficiency.
