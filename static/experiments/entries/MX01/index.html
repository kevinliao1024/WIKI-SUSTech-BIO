SUSTech-BIO 2026 iGEM Team

Purpose

This integrated workflow combines the core molecular biology protocols to construct plasmid variants carrying defined nucleotide substitutions, including the repeat-specific checks needed for PUF variants.

It links M01 (whole-plasmid PCR), M03 (DpnI parental-template removal), M02 (gel analysis and product recovery), M04 (overlap-mediated recircularization), M05 (transformation), M06 (individual-clone sequencing and culture return) and M07 (verified-clone plasmid preparation).

MX01 explains how these modules are selected and connected for site-directed mutagenesis, from the designed mutant sequence to the verified plasmid stock; target-transcript RT-PCR and sequencing readout are covered in MX02.

Before You Start

Design the desired circle and the mutagenic primers

Mark the intended nucleotide changes on the actual parental sequence and translate the desired coding region. Check amino acid substitutions, frame, start/stop codons, linkers and neighboring repeat boundaries before ordering. A mutant name alone is insufficient to define the construct.

Design an F/R pair that produces one full-length linear plasmid product with compatible homology at its two ends. Simulate the PCR product and final circle, count the shared overlap once and inspect the closure junction. Use M04's 15-20 bp terminal overlap, preferably with 40-60% GC. [9] If distant sites do not fit one primer pair, use separate construction rounds or a checked multi-fragment route rather than forcing them into one local pair.

Distinguish the 3' template-binding segment from any nonbinding 5' tail when calculating annealing Tm. Avoid poorly matched 3' ends, strong dimers and alternative binding sites. For repetitive PUF sequence, compare each primer against every repeat copy, especially P4-P7, and favor informative flanking sequence. Design overlap and PCR annealing requirements together. [1]

Plan sequencing coverage across each intended substitution, the closure junction and repeat copies susceptible to unintended changes. Reads anchored to repeat boundaries are more informative than a short read inside a repeated motif.

Prepare the inputs and controls

Prepare separate 10 µM F/R working solutions. Confirm the product length and measured plasmid concentration. Choose one M01 polymerase recipe/program and keep that choice through the run; extension time depends on the full product length.

For method establishment, include a no-template PCR control and a matched no-recombination-Mix DNA control. A compatible plasmid transformation control and no-DNA cell control help localize cell/selection failures. Keep control identities separate from candidate-clone identities.

Keep C117 at -30 to -15°C for storage and assemble on ice. Use M04's rapid single-fragment route below; set 50°C incubation followed by cooling to 4°C or ice. [9]

B. Remove parental template and recover the intended linear product

  1. Check parental host methylation and GATC sites before using DpnI. The parent must be susceptible dam-methylated DNA; newly amplified products are normally unmethylated. DpnI does not remove unwanted PCR amplicons. Use the separate M03 parental-template-removal workflow. [4]

  1. Mix and briefly spin; use the M03 crude-PCR adaptation at 37°C for 15 min. If treating more input, scale the complete setup rather than adding buffer by eye to an entire PCR. When premix/additive compatibility is uncertain, use M03's purified-DNA branch. PCR-buffer carryover is why the crude setup differs from a simple 1X digest. [5]

  2. Run an analytical aliquot using M02, with a ladder above and below the predicted full product size. Choose agarose concentration for that size and the nearest unwanted band; 0.7-1% is a starting choice for many 5-10 kb products. Keep the rest for preparative recovery. Use M02 for loading, running and imaging defects before referring amplification problems to M01 Optimization.

  3. Recover the clearly resolved full-length band using the M02 Omega D2500 workflow. Prepare XP2/SPW correctly, minimize illumination and gel mass, complete the wash/dry steps and elute in 30-50 µL. Quantify the recovered DNA before assembly. D2500's maintained scope is 100 bp-10 kb; choose a verified compatible recovery method for a larger product. [6]

C. Recircularize the plasmid

  1. Check that the two recovered product ends encode the intended complementary overlap and yield the designed circle without duplication or deletion. Calculate and record DNA mass from its measured concentration and the 10 µL input. On ice, combine the DNA and C117 Mix using the M04 rapid recipe below; gently pipette and briefly spin.

This is M04's laboratory-adapted rapid recipe, giving approximately 0.67X CE Mix. The manufacturer reference uses 5 µL of 2X Mix in 10 µL total (1X). If recovery is poor, compare the manufacturer setup with matched DNA mass using M04; document the route used. [9]

  1. Prepare a no-Mix control with 10 µL of the same DNA and 5 µL water. Incubate at 50°C for 5 min, then immediately cool to 4°C or ice. Keep sample and control labels through transformation. [9]

D. Transform and submit individual colonies

  1. Use M05 Route A for the new assembly: thaw 50 µL Trans5α cells on ice and gently add 1-5 µL assembly product. C117 carryover must be at or below one-tenth of the cell volume, so 5 µL is the upper limit at this scale. Use the same transfer volume for the matched no-Mix control. [9]

  2. Keep on ice for 30 min, heat-shock in a 42°C water bath for 45 s and immediately return to ice for 2 min. Add 500 µL antibiotic-free LB and recover at 37°C, 200 rpm for 1 h. Keep the cell product, device, tube geometry and route consistent. [7]

  3. Plate 50-100 µL recovered culture on the matching selective agar and incubate inverted at 37°C overnight, unless the construct/host requires another propagation temperature. Compare sample and control plates before selecting colonies. Use M05 for shared transformation/selection failures.

  4. Follow M06: submit the plate and request independent picking/sequencing of 2-4 isolated colonies per construct. Give each clone a unique ID and request its raw chromatograms, required sequencing coverage and retention of the matching viable culture. Do not pool candidates.

E. Verify the sequence and prepare the selected plasmid

  1. Inspect chromatograms and align high-quality reads to both the parental and intended mutant reference. Confirm every intended nucleotide substitution, translate the affected coding region and review all additional mismatches within the covered region. Check the closure junction and informative repeat boundaries.

  2. For PUF mutants, check every repeat that could have been unintentionally amplified or changed, especially P4-P7. Obtain overlapping reads where repeat placement or critical-site coverage is ambiguous. A correct band size or a clean trace at one intended codon does not establish the full construct. [1,8]

  3. Select only a clone meeting the required sequence coverage and design. Record the verified coordinates and any remaining gaps. Ask for the corresponding viable culture, directly inoculate selective LB using M06 and prepare plasmid using M07. For DC204, match the 5-15 mL preparation to its 12-14 h culture guidance and avoid growth beyond 16 h.

  4. Keep the final mutant name, parental clone ID, primer order, sequence files, coverage record and plasmid stock linked. Retransformation is needed only when a returned plasmid or a deliberate host transfer requires it; receiving a satisfactory matching culture does not require another routine cloning round.

p.s.

Resolve repetitive-sequence specificity before repeating PCR. An apparent full-length product can still contain the wrong-repeat mutation or an unintended rearrangement.

Use working primer dilutions and measured DNA inputs. Change one PCR variable at a time and retain the identity of each tested condition.

Avoid excessive illumination and residual ethanol during product recovery. A clean, quantified eluate is more useful than a large volume of poorly recovered DNA.

Keep each colony independent through sequencing and culture return. Select by sequence and coverage rather than colony size or total colony count.

Expected Outcome

One or more individually labeled bacterial clones may carry the intended substitutions and correct coding context over the required high-quality sequencing coverage. No mutation-recovery rate is guaranteed.

Retain the designed circle, primer order, PCR program, gel image/lane map, assembly inputs, controls and clone-linked traces. Report local verification and any unsequenced regions accurately.

Troubleshooting

Observed problem Check first Action
No full-length band or extra gel products M02 loading/run/imaging; actual primer sites and product length Use M02 first, then M01 Optimization O1/O2. For PUF, compare all repeat matches and simulated product ends.
Few colonies; transformation control grows Recovered DNA, overlap geometry, Mix setup and reaction carryover Quantify input and inspect the circle design. Use the kit-compatible setup; reduce inhibitory carryover or repeat cleanup where indicated.
No-Mix colonies or parental clones Template input, methylation and DpnI compatibility Use M03 DpnI diagnostics. Reprepare affected DNA; unwanted new PCR products require M01 O2 rather than extra DpnI.
No colonies including the transformation control Cell condition, selection and complete M05 route Correct the cell/plate/heat-shock failure before repeating mutagenesis.
Partial, wrong-repeat or extra sequence changes Oligo order, primer specificity and repeat-resolving reads Check against both references; screen independent clones. Redesign unique binding/overlap regions or separate construction rounds if needed.
Mixed peaks or an unresolved critical site Single-colony identity, primer specificity and read coverage Use M06 to re-isolate/rescreen or obtain overlapping reads. Do not accept an ambiguous critical region.
