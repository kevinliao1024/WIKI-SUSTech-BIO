Before You Start Design the homology-arm primers Write the desired circular construct in its final order before ordering primers. For the rapid single-fragment route, the recovered PCR product must contain the sequences and terminal overlaps required to form the intended construct. For one-insert cloning, check both backbone-insert junctions; for several inserts, check every internal junction and both backbone junctions. Use a 15-20 bp terminal overlap at each junction, preferably with 40-60% GC. A primer is written 5'-homology arm + optional retained restriction site + template-specific annealing sequence-3'. Do not count optional restriction-site or residual bases toward the recommended arm length. Calculate PCR-primer Tm from the template-specific part only; 60-65°C is a useful design target, with the PCR program selected for the polymerase in M01. [1] For the forward primer, take the overlap from the upstream neighbor. Write the reverse primer, including its downstream overlap, in the correct reverse-complement orientation. Simulate the PCR products and check matching ends on a common strand; a matching text string in two primer lists alone is not sufficient. For internal junctions, place an overlap from either neighboring fragment onto the other, or split the overlap across both. Avoid repeated junction sequences that could join the wrong partners. Verify orientation, reading frame, start/stop codons and intended linkers in the assembled sequence. Count each shared overlap only once in the final construct. Vazyme CE Design: choose C117 and the matching multi-fragment module [2] Prepare the DNA and incubation program Prepare PCR fragments using M01, remove susceptible parental plasmid with the separate DpnI workflow in M03 when applicable, and recover the intended bands using M02. For restriction-linearized backbones, use M03. Any digestion extension must stay within the limit of the actual enzyme; do not extend FastDigest EcoRI overnight. Use clean eluates without visible gel debris or residual wash solution. Measure individual stocks before combining them for a calculated reaction; blank with the same eluent. Retain the sequence-derived length of each physical input fragment, including its primer-added ends. Keep C117 at -30 to -15°C for storage and assemble reactions on ice. Set 50°C incubation followed by immediate cooling to 4°C or ice. The 2× CE Mix V3 already supplies the reaction enzymes and buffer. [1] Procedure A. Rapid single-fragment recombination 1. Take 10 µL of PCR product purified by agarose gel electrophoresis and gel extraction (M02). Use the recovered DNA eluate for the reaction. 2. The rapid recipe is retained here as used; the calculated multi-fragment route below follows the 1× reference setup. [1] B. Multi-fragment recombination: calculate each input 5. List the backbone and every insert in assembly order with length L (bp) and measured concentration C (ng/µL). Here, n is the number of inserts: C117 supports up to 10 inserts plus one backbone, or 11 DNA pieces in total. [1] For double-stranded DNA of length L, approximate molecular weight MW = 660 × L g/mol. Convert mass to molar amount q using q (pmol) = mass (ng) / (0.66 × L). Conversely, mass (ng) = 0.66 × L × q, and volume (µL) = mass / C. Equal masses of different-length fragments contain different numbers of molecules. 6. Start with approximately 0.03 pmol of each DNA piece, giving a backbone:each insert molar ratio of 1:1. The C117 shortcut is mass (ng) ≈ 0.02 × L (bp). If this falls outside 10-100 ng, use the corresponding 10 ng minimum or 100 ng maximum recommended by C117, then calculate and record the resulting pmol. Mass limits can make the final ratio depart from exact equimolarity. [1] Worked example only: a 4,000 bp backbone plus three inserts. The rounded 0.02 × L rule gives approximately 0.0303 pmol of each physical DNA piece. For the rapid route, prepare a control containing 10 µL of the same gel-purified PCR product and 5 µL water, without CE Mix. For multi-fragment assembly, prepare a backbone-only no-Mix control and, when an insert template carries the same resistance, an insert-only no-Mix control. Match DNA input and final volume to the test. These controls help detect residual circular plasmid; a kit positive control helps assess reagents and transformation. [1] 10. Proceed to M05 for transformation using cloning competent cells and the backbone's selection marker. For chemical transformation, keep assembly-product volume at or below one-tenth of competent-cell volume. C117 products may be stored at -20°C for up to one week if transformation is deferred. [1] 11. Screen individual colonies across the designed junctions, then sequence the junctions and PCR-derived regions needed to confirm the construct. Record the DNA stocks, lengths, masses, volumes, incubation and clone identities so that a successful assembly can be reproduced. p.s. A fixed eluate volume does not fix DNA mass. Check concentrations when a new DNA preparation replaces the previous stock. Expected Outcome The cooled reaction is ready for transformation. Successful clones should give the predicted junction-spanning PCR products and sequence agreement with the designed construct. Neither reaction appearance nor colony growth alone confirms complete, correctly ordered assembly; interpret test colonies alongside the controls.
