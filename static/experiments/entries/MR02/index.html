Before You Start DNA-removal policy and assay controls For this stem-loop branch, omit the 42°C gDNA Eraser pretreatment. Do not add gDNA Eraser, gDNA Eraser Buffer or the supplied RT Primer Mix. The day plan specifies this omission and the user confirmed it was followed. This is the selected experimental adaptation; the RR047A manufacturer workflow includes a separate DNA-removal stage whose buffer enters RT. [1, 4] The day plan gives the following rationale: avoid exposing the DNA RT oligonucleotides to DNA-degrading activity, minimize extra processing of small-RNA samples, and use the artificial stem-loop sequence for the miRNA assays while monitoring DNA background with U6 no-RT controls. This records the design rationale. It does not establish that DNA removal is impossible with custom primers or that all background products are excluded; assess the controls for each assay. [3, 4] Use an extraction method demonstrated to retain the relevant small RNAs; MR01 documents the selected TRIzol route. Keep independent culture samples separate. Assess concentration and possible inhibitor carryover, without treating a NanoDrop ratio or an arbitrary U6 Cq as proof of successful small-RNA recovery. Use dedicated miRNA RT products for the assays below. Oligo(dT)/random-primer mRNA cDNA from M10 does not substitute for these custom stem-loop RT products. Use one qPCR primer pair per well: the shared RT reaction is multiplexed, the qPCR assays are separate. Inspect full amplification and melt curves together with NTC, no-RT and positive controls. Check technical-repeat agreement and assay-specific dilution behavior before accepting numerical results. A similar or single melt peak does not establish target identity; small primer-derived products can resemble the intended short amplicon. [3] 12. Export the raw run, full Cq/amplification/melt data, plate map, dilution record and processing settings. Retain all wells and exclusion reasons. Preserve missing Cq as unavailable; do not assign Cq = 40 or convert a failed control or unqualified assay into zero abundance. 13. For valid dilution points, fit Cq against log10 relative template concentration and calculate E = 10^(-1/slope) - 1. The archived plan proposed 90-110% efficiency and R² at least 0.99 as working acceptance targets; record the observed result and usable range for each assay. A cDNA series qualifies the PCR response within that range, not the whole extraction/RT workflow. [4] 14. Normalize only when a reference is qualified for the intended samples and target/reference efficiencies support the selected calculation. Under appropriate comparative-Cq conditions, calculate ΔCq = Cq(target) - Cq(reference), then ΔΔCq relative to the defined biological calibrator and fold change = 2^(-ΔΔCq). Average acceptable technical repeats within their parent sample; they do not increase biological n. If controls, reference or assay range fail, report the observation and limitation rather than a fold change. MR02 | v1.0 6

p.s. This workflow omits gDNA Eraser pretreatment. U6 primers can amplify genomic DNA, so review no-RT results before using U6 for normalization. If DNA background compromises U6, return to retained RNA for a compatible DNA-removal workflow or qualify a different reference assay. [4] Do not heat an enzyme-containing mixture for oligonucleotide folding. Document whether the optional folding and constant or pulsed RT program were used; the options are not interchangeable evidence of assay success. A late U6 or miRNA Cq can reflect low input, inhibition, poor RT or assay background. It cannot identify failed extraction on its own. Compare qualified controls rather than imposing a universal Cq cutoff. Short products and primer-derived products may share a melt region. Review the complete derivative curve, controls and verified product identity; a clean-looking peak alone is insufficient. [3] Mature-miRNA abundance after mimic delivery does not establish MRE regulation. Use MR03 for mimic delivery and a matched functional comparison for the biological effect. Expected Outcome Expect target-assay amplification with appropriate negative controls, reproducible technical repeats and a usable dilution range when the assays perform adequately. Retain the raw curves and product-identity evidence; a completed reaction does not by itself establish successful miRNA quantification. U6 may be usable only after background and stability assessment. Missing or failed assay results remain unavailable for normalization; the guide does not assume the historical M0D2 run achieved valid endogenous-miRNA quantification. MR02 | v1.0 7
