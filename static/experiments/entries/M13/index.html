DNA Ligation Using T4 DNA Ligase M13 | ver 1.1 | Revised 2026-10-03 SUSTech-BIO 2026 iGEM Team Purpose Join a prepared DNA insert to a linearized plasmid vector using Takara T4 DNA Ligase, then recover and verify recombinant clones. This protocol covers conventional cohesive-end and blunt-end ligation with separately supplied enzyme and 10× buffer. A DNA Ligation Kit or Mighty Mix requires its own formulation and instructions. Prepare restriction-digested DNA using M03 and purify as needed using M02. Check the intended junctions, end compatibility and fragment sizes. Measure DNA after purification. Ligation requires a 5′ phosphate and a 3′ hydroxyl at the bond being sealed. With a dephosphorylated vector, provide a phosphorylated insert. Undigested PCR products and synthetic oligonucleotides may lack 5′ phosphates; arrange phosphorylation when needed and anneal M13 | v1.1 1

Thaw and mix the buffer completely, briefly spin, and keep the enzyme on ice during setup. [2, 3] Procedure A. Calculate DNA inputs 1. Use 50 ng vector and an insert:vector molar ratio of 3:1 as the initial manufacturer example. Optimize within 1:1 to 10:1 when end type, fragment size or vector dephosphorylation warrants it. [1] Insert mass (ng) = vector mass (ng) × insert length (bp) / vector length (bp) × desired insert:vector molar ratio. For each DNA, volume (µL) = mass (ng) / concentration (ng/µL). Calculation example only: a 5,000 bp vector (50 ng) and a 1,000 bp insert at 3:1 require 30 ng insert. Use the purified backbone length, not the length of the original uncut plasmid. B. Assemble the reaction 2. Combine water, buffer and DNA on ice using the following 20 µL reference reaction; add ligase last. At the M05 reference scale, use 1-5 µL reaction per 50 µL chemically competent cells. Include a known plasmid control and a no-DNA control when establishing or troubleshooting the workflow. [5] Takara's standard workflow proceeds directly to transformation without a specified heat-inactivation step. For a pause, store the reaction at -20°C. Remove salts before electroporation using a compatible DNA-cleanup method. [1, 3] 7. Compare sample and control plates. Pick isolated colonies according to M06 and verify the insert, its orientation and both junctions by sequencing; use colony PCR or diagnostic digestion as preliminary screens where useful. [5] p.s. Use molar ratios rather than equal DNA masses. A short annealed oligo can require very little mass; dilute it to a pipettable concentration instead of adding an arbitrary volume. Use the matching ATP-containing buffer. If a previously reliable ligation fails, try a fresh buffer aliquot before increasing enzyme input; ATP deterioration can limit activity. [3] After vector dephosphorylation, confirm insert phosphorylation and removal of active phosphatase. Leaving both partners without a usable 5′ phosphate prevents the required joining reaction. [3] Expected Outcome Successful ligation yields transformants from which sequence-verified recombinant clones can be recovered. More colonies than the vector-only control supports enrichment but does not prove correct assembly. Colony count, gel migration or a positive colony PCR alone cannot establish the complete intended sequence. Retain sample identities, reaction inputs, control observations and verification results.
