ID: M13 · Category: Molecular Biology

M13 | ver 1.1 | Revised 2026-10-03

SUSTech-BIO 2026 iGEM Team

Purpose

Join a prepared DNA insert to a linearized plasmid vector using Takara T4 DNA Ligase, then recover and verify recombinant clones. This protocol covers conventional cohesive-end and blunt-end ligation with separately supplied enzyme and 10× buffer. [1, 2]

Before You Start

Check that the vial is conventional T4 DNA Ligase with a separate 10× buffer. A DNA Ligation Kit or Mighty Mix requires its own formulation and instructions.

Prepare restriction-digested DNA using M03 and purify as needed using M02. Check the intended junctions, end compatibility and fragment sizes. Measure DNA after purification.

Ligation requires a 5′ phosphate and a 3′ hydroxyl at the bond being sealed. With a dephosphorylated vector, provide a phosphorylated insert. Undigested PCR products and synthetic oligonucleotides may lack 5′ phosphates; arrange phosphorylation when needed and anneal complementary oligos before use. [2, 3]

Remove or appropriately inactivate upstream phosphatase before ligation. Keep enzyme stocks at -20°C between uses. Thaw and mix the buffer completely, briefly spin, and keep the enzyme on ice during setup. [2, 3]

Procedure

A. Calculate DNA inputs

  1. Use 50 ng vector and an insert:vector molar ratio of 3:1 as the initial manufacturer example. Optimize within 1:1 to 10:1 when end type, fragment size or vector dephosphorylation warrants it. [1]

Insert mass (ng) = vector mass (ng) × insert length (bp) / vector length (bp) × desired insert:vector molar ratio. For each DNA, volume (µL) = mass (ng) / concentration (ng/µL).

Calculation example only: a 5,000 bp vector (50 ng) and a 1,000 bp insert at 3:1 require 30 ng insert. Use the purified backbone length, not the length of the original uncut plasmid.

B. Assemble the reaction

  1. Combine water, buffer and DNA on ice using the following 20 µL reference reaction; add ligase last. [1]
Component Volume / amount Final condition
10× T4 DNA Ligase Buffer 2 µL 1×
Linearized vector 50 ng; calculated volume Reference DNA input
Insert Calculated volume 3:1 molar ratio initially
T4 DNA Ligase (350 U/µL) 1 µL 350 U per reaction
Nuclease-free water To 20 µL 17 µL minus total DNA volume
Total 20 µL

If combined DNA volume exceeds 17 µL, concentrate and remeasure the DNA. For an impractically small transfer, prepare a measured working dilution and use its concentration in the calculation.

  1. Prepare a vector-only control with the same vector input, buffer and ligase; replace insert volume with water. When investigating background, also prepare vector without insert or ligase, replacing both with water. [4]

  2. Mix gently by pipetting and briefly centrifuge to collect the reaction at the bottom of the tube.

C. Incubate and recover clones

  1. Incubate according to DNA end type. Use the shorter end of the range first for a new cohesive-end reaction; extend within the stated range if recovery is poor after checking controls. [1]
End type Temperature Time
Compatible cohesive ends 16°C 1-5 h
Blunt ends 16°C 1-24 h
  1. Use an aliquot for transformation following M05, preferably its conventional route for a new ligation. At the M05 reference scale, use 1-5 µL reaction per 50 µL chemically competent cells. Include a known plasmid control and a no-DNA control when establishing or troubleshooting the workflow. [5]

Takara's standard workflow proceeds directly to transformation without a specified heat-inactivation step. For a pause, store the reaction at -20°C. Remove salts before electroporation using a compatible DNA-cleanup method. [1, 3]

  1. Compare sample and control plates. Pick isolated colonies according to M06 and verify the insert, its orientation and both junctions by sequencing; use colony PCR or diagnostic digestion as preliminary screens where useful. [5]

p.s.

Use molar ratios rather than equal DNA masses. A short annealed oligo can require very little mass; dilute it to a pipettable concentration instead of adding an arbitrary volume.

Use the matching ATP-containing buffer. If a previously reliable ligation fails, try a fresh buffer aliquot before increasing enzyme input; ATP deterioration can limit activity. [3]

After vector dephosphorylation, confirm insert phosphorylation and removal of active phosphatase. Leaving both partners without a usable 5′ phosphate prevents the required joining reaction. [3]

Expected Outcome

Successful ligation yields transformants from which sequence-verified recombinant clones can be recovered. More colonies than the vector-only control supports enrichment but does not prove correct assembly. Colony count, gel migration or a positive colony PCR alone cannot establish the complete intended sequence. Retain sample identities, reaction inputs, control observations and verification results.

Troubleshooting

Observed problem Check first Suggested action
Few or no colonies, including plasmid control Cell competence, selection and transformation handling Troubleshoot M05 first; a failed transformation control cannot diagnose the ligation.
Few or no colonies, but plasmid control works End compatibility, 5′ phosphates, buffer condition and DNA purity Correct end preparation; use fresh buffer and clean DNA. If those checks pass, extend within the applicable incubation range. [1, 3]
Many colonies in vector-only control Compare vector with and without ligase High no-ligase background suggests uncut plasmid: revisit M03. Background mainly with ligase suggests vector reclosure; consider dephosphorylation for compatible vector ends. [4]
Insert-containing clones have wrong orientation or junctions End design, expected construct map and sequence data For nondirectional ends, screen both orientations. Compare both junctions with the intended sequence and select a verified clone.
Electroporation arcs or gives poor recovery Salt carryover and cell/electroporator requirements Desalt the completed reaction before retrying with an appropriate aliquot; follow the cell-specific electroporation procedure. [3]
