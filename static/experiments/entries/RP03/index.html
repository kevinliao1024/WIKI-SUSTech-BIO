TMP Treatment and Reporter Stabilization Assay RP03 | ver 1.0 | Revised 2026-10-14 SUSTech-BIO 2026 iGEM Team Purpose Test whether trimethoprim (TMP) restores fluorescence from an ecDHFR destabilizing-domain (DD) Reporter in HEK293T cells. Compare WT and InAC with the constitutively fluorescent AC control, each with matched vehicle or TMP treatment. The reference format is a six-well plate with 1 µg Reporter DNA and 2.0 mL treatment medium per well, using 10 µM TMP. This is a Reporter-stabilization assay; fluorescence restoration alone does not establish RNA editing or directly measure protein half-life. Item Identity / Selection Use / Specification Fluorescence readout Microscope and/or flow cytometer RP04: microscopy; RP06: flow acquisition. RP07 is reserved for Reporter data analysis. Before You Start Prepare healthy, matched Reporter-transfected cultures using C04. The original six-well test used 1 µg Reporter DNA per well; WT, AC and InAC each occupied two wells. Transfection doses must be scaled for another vessel rather than copied from a 12-well recipe. [1] Assign each construct to vehicle and +TMP before treatment. Record actual well positions, clone IDs and the transfection time as T = 0. The original one-well-per-condition format is an exploratory screen; use independent culture/transfection wells for reproducible estimates rather than counting flow events as replicates. Check the TMP form and stock concentration against the bottle/lot record. Use a 10 mM DMSO work solution for the main route: final TMP 10 µM and DMSO 0.1% in both arms. Stock solubility differs among products; use 100 mM only when supported for the selected reagent. [1-3] Use a defined treatment interval. The reference schedule is next-day medium exchange at about T + 24 h, followed by 24 h TMP exposure and readout near T + 48 h. If the transfection method requires a different exchange time, specify it and keep the exposure interval identical across wells. [1,4] Procedure A. Prepare TMP in DMSO 1. Calculate the mass for the intended stock: mass (mg) = concentration (mM) × final volume (mL) × MW (g/mol) / 1000. Record the form, lot and actual mass. Apply a purity correction only when the lot certificate provides a quantitative mass fraction; do not automatically treat HPLC area purity as an exact mass fraction. D. Observe, acquire and interpret 10. Check cell morphology and medium clarity during treatment and again before collection. Acquire paired brightfield, EGFP and mCherry images using the same field-selection rule and matched settings within each channel, following RP04. Use images to document the fluorescence response and cell condition. 11. At the endpoint, collect and acquire flow samples using RP06 if flow is the selected readout. Use the same detector settings and acquisition/gating rule across all six conditions. Retain original files and compensation status. Include matched Reporter-negative cells when a current background boundary is needed; preserve the limitation if that control is unavailable. 12. Compare +TMP with vehicle within each construct. Examine the EGFP distribution together with mCherry and cell condition; use a consistently defined, expression-matched population if a normalized comparison is planned. Do not infer compensation from the six dual-color Reporter tubes, or count individual flow events as independent experiments. 13. Look for an EGFP response in DD-containing WT/InAC that differs from the no-DD AC response. Retain original images/FCS, sample map, treatment times and the analysis definition. A single well per condition supports a descriptive pilot; do not attach significance claims or convert this rescue response to an RNA-editing percentage. [1,4] p.s. Keep concentrated TMP solutions in DMSO rather than using a high-concentration aqueous intermediate. Check clarity after dilution into medium and use freshly prepared treatment media. Precipitation makes the delivered dose uncertain. [1-3] Match DMSO between TMP and vehicle. Do not assume that 0.1% DMSO is biologically inert. If solvent effects are a concern, include untreated cultures as an additional comparison while retaining the matched vehicle pair. If reducing DMSO is necessary, a 20 mM DMSO work solution is an alternative: 200 µL of a supported 100 mM stock + 800 µL DMSO; then 3.3 µL work solution + 6596.7 µL medium per 6.60 mL arm. Use 3.3 µL pure DMSO in the vehicle arm. Both arms contain 0.05% DMSO, with 10 µM TMP only in +TMP. Use a calibrated pipette for 3.3 µL and one route consistently; this alternative is not an established optimized condition. [1] Interpret the AC control alongside WT/InAC. A response shared by all constructs, loss of cells, or a changing mCherry distribution calls for reassessment before attributing fluorescence changes to DD stabilization. Use the actual stock label for storage and the actual device instructions for filtration. A clear solution and a membrane-material name alone do not establish correct concentration, sterility or compatibility of the complete filter assembly. [2,3,5] RP03 | v1.0 4

Expected Outcome Obtain matched vehicle/TMP fluorescence records for WT, AC and InAC, accompanied by cell-condition checks and treatment metadata. An increase in WT/InAC EGFP relative to their vehicle wells, with a smaller response in AC under comparable sampling and expression conditions, is consistent with TMP-responsive DD behavior. No fixed rescue magnitude or saturation threshold is required. Report the observed distributions and replicate structure. This assay tests Reporter stabilization behavior; target-transcript sequencing is needed separately to assess RNA editing.
