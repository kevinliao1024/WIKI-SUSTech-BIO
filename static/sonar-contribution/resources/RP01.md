# Custom Oligo Design for Target-Site Recognition Window



RP01 | ver 1.0 | Revised 2026-10-13

SUSTech-BIO 2026 iGEM Team

## Purpose

Design complementary DNA oligonucleotides to insert a target-site recognition window into the universal reporter. Prepare WT, pre-edited AC, and inactive INAC reporter sequences with the intended reading frame and directional cloning junctions.

This guide covers sequence design, checks, and synthesis specifications. Oligo annealing and reporter construction are covered in RP02.

## Before You Start

Fix the transcript, target C, and editor footprint before choosing insert boundaries. Design in the coding/sense DNA representation; write every ordered strand 5' to 3'.

Open the full construction vector. The 2,027-bp universal GenBank reference describes the cassette; additional BsaI sites in a complete plasmid must be resolved before BsaI-based RP02 construction. [1,2]

The worked example uses coding-strand fusion sequences CAGC (EGFP side) and GGCA (DHFR side). The reverse oligo starts with TGCC, the reverse complement of GGCA. Recalculate the ends if the acceptor changes. [1]

## Procedure

### A. Define the target window

1. Mark the target C in the reference transcript and map the complete recognition sequence and any intervening spacer. Preserve their sequence, orientation, and spacing when transferring them into the reporter. Binding-site length and editing geometry depend on the editor; they should not be inferred solely from the oligo length. [5]

2. Use the APOE4 example below as a worked design: retain 12 nucleotides immediately 5' of the target C and two nucleotides immediately 3' of it, giving a 15-nt core. The original design partitions this as the 10-nt recognition sequence ATGGAGGACG, the 2-nt spacer TG, and the target codon CGA. [1]

| Element | DNA sequence (5' to 3') | Position in the core |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Upstream block | ATGGAGGACGTG | 1-12; recognition sequence plus spacer |
| Target nucleotide | C | 13; first base of the target codon |
| Downstream bases | GA | 14-15; complete the CGA codon |
| Complete WT core | ATGGAGGACGTGCGA | 15 nt; excludes cloning overhangs |

3. Make the edited base produce a stop codon in the actual reporter reading frame. In this example, the native APOE4 arginine codon CGC is synonymously changed to CGA; RNA editing then converts CGA to UGA. WT denotes the unedited reporter, whose core therefore differs from the native target at the codon's third base. Preserve the recognition footprint and flag all engineered substitutions in the design file. [1]

### B. Design matched reporter controls

4. Create AC by replacing only the intended C with T in the DNA sequence. Keep the same flanking sequence, length, junctions, and backbone as WT, so AC contains the pre-edited TGA codon.

5. Create INAC with a disrupted recognition sequence and a codon that cannot become a stop codon through a single C-to-U change. In the worked example, the 15-nt INAC core is ACGTCAGTCATGGCC: its terminal GCC codon remains a sense codon after any single C-to-U change. Retain the same insert length and cloning interfaces as WT. [1]

| Reporter | Core sequence (5' to 3') | Intended role |
| --- | --- | --- |
| WT | ATGGAGGACGTGCGA | Unedited substrate; CGA encodes Arg and permits translation into the downstream domain. |
| AC | ATGGAGGACGTGTGA | Pre-edited reporter; TGA places a stop upstream of the DHFR domain. |
| INAC | ACGTCAGTCATGGCC | Reporter background control; both recognition sequence and terminal codon are changed. |

AC provides a pre-edited reference, rather than a guaranteed fluorescence ceiling. INAC is a reporter-sequence control; a catalytically inactive editor is a separate control. Because INAC changes both recognition and codon sequence, it cannot by itself isolate the contribution of recognition-site binding.

### C. Add directional cloning ends

6. For a core sequence I, write the forward oligo as CAGC + I and the reverse oligo as TGCC + reverse-complement(I), with both strands written 5' to 3'. Only the core is fully paired after annealing; the two 4-nt 5' extensions form the cloning ends. These oligos already supply the required cohesive ends and do not need added BsaI recognition sites. [1,2]

| Oligo | Complete sequence (5' to 3') | Length |
| --- | --- | --- |
| WT-F (APOE4-F) | CAGCATGGAGGACGTGCGA | 19 nt |
| WT-R (APOE4-R) | TGCCTCGCACGTCCTCCAT | 19 nt |
| AC-F | CAGCATGGAGGACGTGTGA | 19 nt |
| AC-R | TGCCTCACACGTCCTCCAT | 19 nt |
| INAC-F | CAGCACGTCAGTCATGGCC | 19 nt |
| INAC-R | TGCCGGCCATGACTGACGT | 19 nt |

7. Reconstruct the complete assembled coding-strand junction, including vector-derived bases on both sides. For WT it is GGCAGC | ATGGAGGACGTGCGA | GGCAGC, translating to Gly-Ser | Met-Glu-Asp-Val-Arg | Gly-Ser. The overhang bases belong to these junctions and must not be counted as extra sequence added a second time. Translate across both junctions and into the downstream domain.

### D. Consider flanking context and verify the design

8. For new designs, consider short transition segments on both sides of the core to reduce unfavorable pairing with adjacent reporter RNA. Keep them outside the recognition footprint, spacer, and target codon, and identical across WT, AC, and INAC. Add complete codons when the existing core starts at a codon boundary, then recheck translation. Select segments by their sequence context; adding flanks does not guarantee improved accessibility. [6]

9. Check the complete intended sequence for reverse-complement agreement, correct insertion orientation, both junctions, and the desired translation. WT and INAC must lack unintended stops before the downstream domain; AC and the simulated edited WT must stop at the intended codon. For BsaI-based construction, scan the full retained vector, insert, and new junctions for GGTCTC and GAGACC. Resolve additional sites by a compatible redesign or assembly route before construction. [2]

10. Optionally screen the assembled reporter RNA (T to U) with RNAfold at 37 C using -p -T 37. Inspect pairing probabilities over the footprint and target C, including surrounding reporter sequence; compare candidates with identical context and settings. RNAplfold -u can additionally estimate a region's unpaired probability. Save the sequence, software version, and settings. Predictions guide design and require experimental validation. [6-8]

### E. Prepare the synthesis order

11. Order each strand as single-stranded DNA with its complete 5'-to-3' sequence, including the 4-nt cloning end. Specify a 5' phosphate on both strands and an unblocked 3'-OH. If unphosphorylated oligos are selected instead, include a T4 polynucleotide kinase phosphorylation step before ligation in RP02. [3]

12. Select PAGE purification for the cloning oligos; consider HPLC when the provider recommends it for the required purity, yield, or modification compatibility. Choose a synthesis scale that supplies the planned assemblies. Save the ordered sequences, strand names, lengths, modification and purification choices, annotated assembled sequence, and the target-site mapping. [4]

## p.s.

Count from the edited base, not from the first nucleotide in the recognition motif: this example has 12 bases before C and two after it. A different editor footprint may require a longer core.

An additional flanking segment belongs outside the recognition-to-target interval. Adding bases inside that interval changes editing geometry even if the overall reading frame is preserved.

Keep the 4-nt cohesive ends distinct from PCR homology tails. The reverse oligo is reverse-complemented before its TGCC end is added; reverse-complementing the entire forward oligo gives the wrong end arrangement.

Screen the complete construction vector before ordering. A clean cassette or a filename containing 'universal' does not establish that the rest of the vector is free of BsaI sites. [2]

## Expected Outcome

The design package contains three matched reporter cores, six synthesis-ready DNA sequences, and an annotated assembled sequence for each variant. The example gives a 15-nt core and two 19-nt oligos per reporter, with correct directional ends and the intended WT/AC/INAC translation states.

Sequence checks establish design compatibility. Reporter construction, fluorescence behavior, and RNA editing must be assessed experimentally; a fluorescence change alone does not establish target-site editing.

## Troubleshooting

| Observed design problem | Check first | Action |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Reverse strand does not give the intended cloning ends | Whether the entire forward strand, including CAGC, was reverse-complemented | Reverse-complement only the core, then prepend TGCC. Recheck the annealed duplex against the acceptor. |
| Frameshift or unintended stop in the simulated junction | Complete junction sequence, duplicated overhangs, codon boundary, and added segment lengths | Rebuild the complete coding junction and translate it. Adjust only permitted flanking bases while preserving the recognition-to-target geometry. |
| Editing the marked C does not produce a stop | Target base position and the actual in-frame codon | Use a compatible edit-to-stop codon; in this example, design CGA so C-to-U gives UGA. Recheck all intended sequence changes. |
| Restriction scan shows extra cuts | GGTCTC and GAGACC in the full retained vector, insert, and junctions | Resolve incompatible sites before RP02. Use permissible substitutions or a compatible assembly route; do not rely on the cassette-only map. |
| Optional RNAfold check predicts pairing over the core | Transcript context, target coordinates, and identical settings across candidates | Compare permitted external transition segments or synonymous flanking variants while preserving the core. Validate promising candidates experimentally. [6-8] |
